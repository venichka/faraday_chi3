# SiC run: all-optical Faraday rotation (χ⁽⁵⁾)

12 November 2026

## Goal

See a rotation of the probe polarisation that appears only when both pumps overlap in time, with counter-rotating (σ⁺σ⁻) pumps, and that nearly disappears with co-rotating (σ⁺σ⁺) pumps.

Do the L = 3.2 µm sample first: it needs the fewest scans. Then repeat the run on L = 4.8 µm as a second, independent observation.

## Settings

| | L = 3.2 µm (first) | L = 4.8 µm (second) |
|---|---|---|
| Probe, 45° linear, weak (≤ 10⁻⁴ of the pump intensity) | 850.2 nm | 794.2 nm |
| Pumps, σ⁺ and σ⁻ | 1655.9 and 1695.2 nm | 1515.2 and 1569.9 nm |
| Peak intensity per pump, both equal | 3 × 10¹¹ W/cm², never above 5 × 10¹¹ | same |
| Expected rotation | 0.056° (1.9 × 10⁻³ of V+H) | 0.059° (2.1 × 10⁻³ of V+H) |
| Fast fringe on top (averages out over scans) | 0.10° | 0.19° |
| Scans of the main measurement | 10 | 15 |
| Co-rotating pumps: rotation, fringe | 0.011°, 0.012° | below 0.001° both |
| Each pump alone | 0.002–0.004° | 0.001–0.003° |
| Light the pumps can generate near the probe | 828.0, 837.7, 847.6 nm | 757.6, 771.0, 785.0 nm |

For both samples: chopper on the pump that travels with the probe, 500 Hz, synchronised to the laser; balanced broadband detection, no filter in the probe path.

The wavelengths are from the design stack. The real ones come from step 1.

## Run order

1. **Linear spectrum** of the sample at the spot to be used, 700–1900 nm, step ≤ 1 nm. Send it to us; we return the exact probe and pump wavelengths within a day.
2. **Set the pumps**, raising the intensity in steps from 1 × 10¹¹ to 3 × 10¹¹ W/cm². If V+H drops by more than about 5 % and stays down at lower power, stop and move to a fresh spot.
3. **Delay-axis calibration**: scan an interference of known wavelength on the same stage and compare its period with λ/2 of travel. The SIN51 data only fitted with the delay axis stretched by 1.12.
4. **Coarse delay scan**, ±2 ps in 20 fs steps, to find the overlap centre τ₀.
5. **Main measurement**: τ₀ ± 200 fs in 10 fs steps, σ⁺σ⁻ pumps, 10 scans (15 on L = 4.8 µm).
6. **Probe blocked**: the same scan, as many scans as the main measurement, alternated with the main ones. Any signal here is light generated by the pumps, not rotation.
7. **Co-rotating pumps (σ⁺σ⁺)**: the same scan, as many scans as the main measurement. On L = 3.2 µm the signal should fall to about 10 % and the fringe about 8×; on L = 4.8 µm both should disappear.
8. **Each pump alone**, chopper on the pump that is on, 2 scans each.
9. **Intensity series**: 2 × 10¹¹ and 5 × 10¹¹ W/cm² per pump, 10 scans each.

If the signal is weak after step 5, raise both pumps together towards 5 × 10¹¹ before changing anything else.

## What is different on L = 4.8 µm

- **The fringe is twice as large** for the same rotation, so it needs about 15 scans instead of 10.
- **More intensity helps here.** From 3 to 5 × 10¹¹ W/cm² the rotation grows faster than the fringe (rotation / fringe 0.32 → 0.41). On L = 3.2 µm it is the opposite (0.56 → 0.45), so there raise the intensity only if the signal is too weak.
- **The probe loses more polarisation at high intensity**: about 4 % at 5 × 10¹¹, against 1.5 % on L = 3.2 µm.
- **The co-rotating test is cleaner**: rotation and fringe should both vanish, not just fall.
- **Pump-generated light sits closer to the probe**: the second harmonic of the 1569.9 nm pump is at 785.0 nm, 9 nm from the probe. The probe-blocked scans matter most on this sample.
- **It needs its own linear spectrum** (step 1) and its own wavelengths.
- **Do not use the probe modes at 689–695 nm**: the fringe there is 10–16 times the rotation.

## Please

- Save every scan as its own file; do not average in the instrument.
- Record per point: delay, stage read-back, lock-in X and Y, V+H, time.
- Record per file: wavelengths, powers, spot sizes, pulse durations, pump helicities, which pump is delayed, V−H and V+H gains, lock-in settings.
- Do not chop the delayed pump: its own rotation then mixes into the overlap signal.
- Do not set the probe by wavelength alone: find the cavity mode from the measured spectrum.
